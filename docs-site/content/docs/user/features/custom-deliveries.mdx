With **Satisfier** (vsatisfy) installed, your assistant can see this week's custom delivery requests and deliver them for you: one client at a time, or the whole week as a background job.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `get_custom_deliveries` | The deliveries left this week, and per client: unlocked or not, rank, satisfaction, the deliveries to the next rank, deliveries left, and the three requests (craft, gather, fish) with the item, the bonus, how many it still takes, how many you carry, their level and the job that makes them |
| `deliver_custom_delivery` | Delivers one client's request and waits until it is done |
| `do_custom_deliveries` | Does this week's deliveries for all clients, one after another, as a background job |
| `stop_custom_delivery` | Stops what Satisfier is doing |

> "What do my custom delivery clients want this week?"
>
> "Do my custom deliveries."
>
> "Deliver the crafting request to Zhloe."

## How a request is delivered

Each client asks for three things a week: something to **craft**, to **gather** and to **fish**. Each delivery counts once against the weekly allowance shared by all clients (12).

- **Crafting**: Satisfier buys the ingredient from the client's vendor, crafts with **Artisan** and turns the items in.
- **Gathering**: Satisfier has **Questionable** gather the items, and Questionable turns them in. When the items are already in your bags, XIV MCP has Questionable turn them in directly: Satisfier's own turn-in doesn't work for every client (Tiisol Ja).
- **Fishing**, only when you ask for it: XIV MCP catches the fish with **AutoHook** (switch to Fisher, bait, the spot, fishing until there are enough), then Satisfier turns them in. Spearfishing requests aren't supported yet.

Without `kind`, the request with this week's bonus comes first, then the crafting request (crafters' scrips buy the more valuable materia), and the gathering one (Miner or Botanist, whichever it needs) when crafting isn't possible. Fishing is never chosen unless you ask for it.

## Ranks

Every delivery raises the client's satisfaction by the same amount, for all three requests and with or without the bonus: 30 at rank 1, 80 at rank 2, 130 at rank 3 and 180 at rank 4. The next rank needs 90, 480, 780 and 1,080. So rank 1 takes 3 deliveries, and ranks 2, 3 and 4 take 6 each, a whole week of deliveries to that client. A rank-up brings new requests.

`get_custom_deliveries` shows per client below the top rank the satisfaction one delivery adds (`satisfactionPerDelivery`) and the deliveries to the next rank (`deliveriesToRankUp`). How many a request "still takes" is not the way to the next rank: it is the client's deliveries left this week, or fewer when the client ranks up before that.

## Levels

Satisfier crafts and gathers with the job its **Job choice** setting picks: the crafter and gatherer you chose (Specific), your current job (Current), or your crafter and gatherer with the lowest or the highest level (Lowest XP, Highest XP). It does not look at the request's level. Satisfier shows the crafter and gatherer to choose only with Specific; with the other choices it falls back to the last ones chosen there (Carpenter and Miner unless you changed them), for example when no crafter is unlocked. With Current, Satisfier still crafts with the chosen crafter, and gathers with your job when the delivery starts if that is Miner or Botanist. In the weekly job a crafting or fishing delivery changes your job, so a gathering delivery after it uses the chosen gatherer, which can be another one than the plan expected.

`get_custom_deliveries` shows per request its level, the job that makes it and that job's level. For crafting the level is the recipe's, for gathering the lowest gathering point that has the item (or the item's own level, if that is higher), for fishing the fish's or its spot's. A job can make a request somewhat below its level:

- A crafter can start recipes up to 5 levels above its own. A level 71 Armorer crafted a level 72 recipe at the client's top collectability tier.
- A gatherer finds gathering points up to 4 levels above its own, as gathering guides say. Checked in game only 5 levels above (hidden) and 2 below (visible); 1 to 4 levels above is not checked yet. Whether an item above the gatherer's level can be gathered at a point it finds is not known, so XIV MCP does not refuse for it.

Further below than that, XIV MCP does not start a crafting or gathering delivery: it says so before anything is bought, and the weekly job delivers another kind to that client instead. Within it, the delivery goes ahead. Whether the items reach the collectability the client asks for depends on your gear as much as on the job's level, so XIV MCP does not guess it. What counts is what Satisfier makes: everything the request still takes this week, also past the scrip cap, less the items already in your bags. It counts crafted items from collectability 1 and gathered ones from the client's lowest tier. Fishing levels are only shown: no margin is known for them, so XIV MCP does not judge them.

## The weekly job

`do_custom_deliveries` plans every client's remaining deliveries within the weekly allowance, then delivers them one client after another. `kind` uses one kind for everyone, `clients` limits it to some clients. `get_job` follows it.

The plan:

- **Clients below their top rank come first**, as their deliveries still raise their rank.
- **Clients whose achievements are done go last**, even with a bonus: their deliveries no longer count toward anything but scrips.
- **Then clients with a bonus** this week, on a request that can be delivered (a fishing bonus only counts when you ask for fishing).
- **Then clients whose achievements need the most deliveries**: each client's "The Customer Is Always Right" achievements count the deliveries to them, up to 150. XIV MCP asks the game for that count, as the Achievements window does. The others follow in the game's order.
- **Per client: the bonus request, then crafting, gathering for the rest.** A request stops where the scrip cap would be passed (the cap counts every delivery in the plan), or where it takes fewer deliveries than the client still does (Tiisol Ja at rank 4 takes three of each). The next kind then takes the client's remaining deliveries.
- **A request the job cannot make is left out** (see [Levels](#levels)), and the next kind takes its place. `leftOutForLevel` names the requests the plan would otherwise have delivered, and why.

The plan shows per client whether it is still below its top rank (`ranksUp`), which of its deliveries reaches the next rank (`rankUpDelivery`, none when the planned ones fall short), whether the request has the bonus, and the deliveries its achievements still need (`achievementDeliveriesLeft`).

A cutscene after a turn-in (a rank-up, the week's last delivery) is waited out, the client's dialogue after it is clicked through, and the rank-up rewards window it ends in is accepted, so the character is free when the delivery is done; only a cutscene that keeps the character for over three minutes stops the delivery. To watch a rank-up story yourself, see [Watching a rank-up](#watching-a-rank-up). After a gathering delivery Questionable is stopped, so it does not go on to the client's new sidequest by itself.

While a delivery runs, Satisfier's window stays open: Satisfier keeps its client data up to date while it is shown.

While a delivery runs, **YesAlready** is paused (TextAdvance is not). Its "Custom Deliveries" option turns items in the moment a delivery window loads, even while the window is still hidden behind a client's dialogue, as with Tiisol Ja. The conversation then runs ahead without the window, Satisfier waits for a window that never shows, and anything that touches the hidden window can crash the game. YesAlready resumes as soon as the delivery is done.

## Watching a rank-up

Satisfier hands in every delivery itself, also the one that ranks a client up, and the client's story after it is clicked through. To watch that story, ask your assistant to leave the rank-up story to you (`player_ranks_up`, off unless you ask for it):

- The deliveries before the rank-up go in as usual, always with Satisfier. For a gathering request whose items are already in your bags, XIV MCP otherwise has Questionable turn them in, but Questionable would hand in the rank-up item too. Satisfier's own turn-in does not work for every client (Tiisol Ja), so such a delivery can fail with this option on.
- For the rank-up delivery, the item is still made and your character is taken to the client. Satisfier closes the client's menu after the deliveries before it.
- XIV MCP then hands it in without Satisfier, as you would: it talks to the client, picks the request, puts the item in and presses Hand Over. Satisfier is not used for this one because it clicks through every line of dialogue while it turns in.
- From Hand Over on, the story is yours: XIV MCP waits until you are out of it, and clicks or closes nothing in it, also when the job is paused or cancelled then, or the delivery fails.
- If the client's deliveries left do not go down within 10 seconds of Hand Over, the delivery fails and says why, so your assistant can run it again. When the hand-over window is still open then, the click did not land and the window is closed; otherwise nothing is touched, in case the story started.
- If the delivery goes in but the client does not reach the next rank (an item below the client's top collectability tier gives less satisfaction), the delivery ends and says so.
- If XIV MCP cannot get to Hand Over (the game asks a question first, or a window does not open), it tells you in chat and with a notification to hand it in yourself. After 30 minutes without a hand-in it stops waiting; in a conversation you started after being asked, it waits up to 2 hours. Running the delivery again makes the item again if it is gone, takes you back to the client first, then tries again. Running it again after the client ranked up does nothing.
- XIV MCP waits as long as the story holds your character after the hand-in. Only when the game holds it with no window left for a minute does XIV MCP stop waiting and say so. The delivery is done then.
- A rank-up only counts when it fits in this week's deliveries left. When there are fewer left than the client needs, nothing is left to you.
- Lowering Satisfier's count (below) and the hand-in without Satisfier are workarounds for what Satisfier does on its own. The delivery's result names the Satisfier version it ran with and the workarounds it used (`workarounds`), and both are on [Plugins XIV MCP uses](/docs/user/plugins#custom-deliveries), with the Satisfier version they were last checked against.

The weekly job does this for every client that ranks up. After each rank-up it plans the rest of the week again, as the client has new requests then. While the job waits for you, your assistant's other actions in the game are refused, as during any job step. If TextAdvance is on, it still moves the story on by itself.

To have Satisfier stop before the rank-up delivery, XIV MCP lowers Satisfier's count of the deliveries that client takes this week while it runs. Satisfier then makes every item, walks to the client and turns in only the deliveries before the rank-up. `get_custom_deliveries` still shows the real numbers meanwhile. The count is put back as soon as Satisfier is done, and when XIV MCP is turned off or updated; a Satisfier still running then is stopped first. When the rank-up is the first delivery, the item is in your bags and your character stands at the client (or the client's delivery window is open), Satisfier is not started at all.

## Scrip caps

Deliveries pay scrips, and each scrip has a cap (4,000). When a turn-in would take a scrip over its cap, the game asks first, and neither Satisfier nor Questionable answers that question. So XIV MCP delivers only as many as fit under the cap, counting the most a delivery can pay. When none fit, it doesn't start and says which scrip is in the way: spend some first, for example on materia. `get_custom_deliveries` shows per request how many fit (`fitUnderScripCap`) and which scrip limits it, and `get_currencies` lists every scrip with its cap.

If the game asks a question during a turn-in anyway, the delivery stops right away, answers No and reports what it asked.
